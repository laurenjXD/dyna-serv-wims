## Multi-item Pick Lists-tab workflow (2026-08-25) — approved

The Product Owner approved a change from direct, single-item Stock View generation to a table-like draft in Master Inventory's **Pick Lists** tab:

- One draft belongs to exactly one destination Organization and one Inventory Model, but can contain many item-code rows.
- Each row selects its authoritative lot/location source and quantity in boxes; item/customer codes, description, UOM, and SPQ are displayed from Master Inventory.
- Editing the draft changes no inventory state. One **Generate Pick List** command validates and reserves every row atomically, producing one `pick_list` number and one committed multi-line snapshot.
- The operational Pick List PDF is generated from that committed snapshot and becomes available for preview/download/print. It is never a source of reservation authority, and a document-rendering failure does not reverse a successful commitment.
- The committed list enters **To Pick**; after its non-scan pick confirmation it enters **Dispatch**, where the per-box QR scan gate remains unchanged.

This amends `08-outgoing-withdrawal-and-two-stage-commitment` and `10-pick-list-and-acknowledgement-receipt`. Both required approvals were granted in conversation on 2026-08-25; implementation is authorized.

## Direct-to-dispatch generated pick lists (2026-08-25) — approved

Both approvers authorized removal of the separate in-application “Confirm boxes staged” step. The Pick Lists queue replaces “Go to Pick” with **View / PDF** and an explicit **Dispatch** action. QR scanning remains exclusively at Dispatch.

## WRR-style shared-QR dispatch reconciliation (2026-08-25) — approved

Both approvers clarified that there is one shared QR per item/lot context, not a unique QR printed for every box. Dispatch now follows WRR reconciliation: it accepts the matching shared item QR/code or lot QR repeatedly, automatically selects the corresponding incomplete Pick List line, increments it one box per accepted scan, and stops at the committed quantity. A lot QR targets its exact lot/location line; a shared item QR that appears on multiple lines counts the first incomplete match. Wrong item/lot/location values and over-quantity scans remain blocked, but duplicate unique-box detection is intentionally removed because a unique box label does not exist.

## WRR-owned input and Master Inventory-backed pick-list fields (2026-08-24) — pending reapproval

The Product Owner clarified the field ownership for the supplied Warehouse Receiving Report and Pick List templates.

- **WRR is the only CRUD surface.** While a WRR is staged, authorized receiving users may create, edit, and delete the commercial/header data (Organization, invoice/CIPL, MAWB/MBL, and IP) and expected-line data (selected item, supplier/Dyna-Serv/customer codes, shipping lot, manufacture date, expected in-transit quantity/UOM, and remarks). The WRR detail and print routes are read-only projections of that persisted data.
- **Actual receipt is not free-form CRUD.** Actual received quantity/UOM and received date are derived from accepted scans and receipt confirmation; allowing users to type those values would bypass the inventory transaction safety boundary. Manufacture date and remarks require new nullable WRR-line fields; manufacture date is copied to the confirmed lot and remarks remain non-quantity evidence.
- **Pick lists have no editable line data.** Their item code, customer item code, description, lot, location, quantity, SPQ, and package count are selected from Master Inventory by the approved allocation/commitment flow and frozen in `pick_list_items`. Party name/address is read from the selected Organization snapshot. Totals are derived from the snapshot.
- **Delivery-only template fields are not invented.** Client D.R. No., DGC D.R. No., delivery date, and delivery instructions cannot be derived from Master Inventory and remain unavailable in v1 until an approved owning workflow exists; `19-dispatch-scheduling-and-delivery-tracking` remains deferred. The pick-list number must not be misrepresented as a delivery-receipt number.

This affects `01-core-data-model` (new WRR-line persistence), `07-incoming-receiving` (WRR form/read-only/print contract), and `10-pick-list-and-acknowledgement-receipt` (document field/source contract). Both approvals were granted in conversation on 2026-08-24; all three specifications are **Approved** and implementation is authorized.

## Vercel/Supabase region mismatch — root cause of app-wide slowness (2026-08-17)

Reported live: "why does it take so long to click and load everything." Confirmed with hard data, not inference: `DATABASE_URL`'s host is `aws-0-ap-northeast-1.pooler.supabase.com` (Supabase database in Tokyo), but the Vercel runtime log shown earlier this session (the successful `GET /` request) showed the function itself "Routed to Washington, D.C., USA (iad1)" — Vercel's default region, with no region config anywhere in the repo pinning it elsewhere. Every database round trip — every page's session/grants check via middleware + `requirePermission()`, plus every page's own queries (the dashboard alone fires ~10 in parallel) — was crossing the Pacific and back, on top of `prepare: false`'s already-higher per-query cost (required for Supabase's pgbouncer transaction-mode pooler, but it disables server-side statement-plan caching).

**Fix**: `export const preferredRegion = "hnd1"` (Vercel's Tokyo region) added to `app/layout.tsx`, which applies to every route nested under it by Next.js App Router convention on Vercel. The one Route Handler in the app (`app/(authenticated)/inventory/export/route.ts`, the Stock View Excel export) isn't part of the layout tree and doesn't inherit that config, so it got its own identical `preferredRegion` export.

**Not yet verified against real latency** — this requires an actual Vercel deployment to confirm the improvement; `tsc`/build only confirm the config is syntactically valid and doesn't break anything, not that it fixes the latency. Also not yet confirmed: whether the current Vercel plan supports region selection (Hobby tier only runs in the default US region regardless of `preferredRegion`; Pro tier and above honor it) — if the plan doesn't support it, this config is a harmless no-op, not a regression, but won't help either. Worth confirming plan tier before assuming this is resolved.

## Production incident: dashboard 500 on every sign-in, caused by the administrator RBAC fix exposing a pre-existing bug (2026-08-17)

Reported live: after the administrator RBAC amendment below shipped, every sign-in landed on a "server-side exception" page. Confirmed via Vercel runtime logs (`GET /` on the current production deployment, digest matching):

```
Error: Failed query: SELECT date_trunc($1, pl.created_at) AS period, ...
  [cause]: TypeError: The "string" argument must be of type string or an
  instance of Buffer or ArrayBuffer. Received an instance of Date
```

**Root cause chain**: `/` (the dashboard, landing page after every sign-in) calls `getPickListQtyAndCbmTrend()` from `lib/analytics/queries/outbound.ts` only when `hasPickListAccess` is true. Before the administrator RBAC amendment (same day, below), `administrator` lacked `pick_list.read`, so this call was always skipped for admin sessions (dormant, `Promise.resolve([])` fallback) — the underlying bug was never exercised by that role. The RBAC fix granted `administrator` the same `pick_list.*` capabilities as `supervisor`, which is exactly what was asked for and is correct — but it also newly exercised this code path for admin sessions for the first time, surfacing an independent, pre-existing defect: every function in `outbound.ts` interpolated raw JS `Date` objects directly into a Drizzle `sql\`\`` template, which this deployment's Drizzle/postgres.js combination cannot serialize as a bind parameter for this query shape. This bug predates today's session entirely (part of the original dashboard-upgrade work) and would have affected `warehouse_staff`/`supervisor` sessions identically all along — it just took the RBAC fix to expose it for administrator.

**Fix**: every `Date` range value in `lib/analytics/queries/outbound.ts` (`getPickListVolumeTrend`, `getPickListQtyAndCbmTrend`, `getDispatchRate`, `getTopDispatchedItems`, `getCommitmentDuration`) is now converted to an ISO string (`.toISOString()`) before interpolation, so the bound parameter is always an unambiguous string. `inventory.ts` and `heatmap.ts` (the dashboard's other analytics queries) don't interpolate raw dates and were unaffected.

**Regression coverage added**: `lib/analytics/queries/__tests__/outbound.test.ts` (new — this module had zero prior test coverage, which is why the bug shipped undetected). Compiles each function's built query through Drizzle's own `PgDialect` (the same compilation step the real driver uses) and asserts no bound parameter is ever a raw `Date` instance — verified directly (via a standalone `PgDialect.sqlToQuery` check) that this is exactly the failure shape that broke production. Full suite green after: `tsc`, 1417/1417 tests, build.

**Not yet deployed** — this fix is committed locally only as of this entry; it needs to reach `main` and redeploy before sign-in works again on production.

## Three RLS write-policy gaps closed after the A2 rbac-rls-reviewer pass (2026-08-11)

A `rbac-rls-reviewer` pass over the app-wide RLS session-binding rollout ("A2" -- server actions now run each mutation under the caller's own RLS-bound session rather than a bypassing connection) found three write-policy gaps left over from earlier, narrower-scoped migrations. ## `07` receiving reversed: no pre-scan putaway location, no single end-of-WRR commit gate (2026-08-10)

**Supersedes:** the "`07` receipt commit — explicit per-line putaway location selected (2026-08-09)" entry below, in full. That entry recorded the Product Owner's prior decision that a `store`-disposition WRR line records its `putaway_location_id` **before** receiving begins, and that a WRR commits in one all-or-nothing transaction once every line is scanned/resolved. The Product Owner has now reversed that decision in conversation, on the stated reasoning that a location cannot be meaningfully suggested for an item the system doesn't yet know is arriving — pre-selecting a location at WRR-staging time, before anyone has scanned anything, was backwards.

**Important, stated honestly**: the 2026-08-09 decision was not just approved — it was already implemented and, per the "Production migration verification — document/RLS chain and C1 putaway location (2026-08-10)" entry below, already **applied to the linked production Supabase project** (migration `0020_wrr_item_putaway_location.sql`, confirmed live: `public.wrr_items.putaway_location_id` nullable `uuid`, FK to `locations(id)`, indexed). This reversal does **not** undo that migration — `putaway_location_id` stays nullable on `wrr_items`, exactly as it already is, and no new migration is proposed by this pass. What changes is *when* the column is populated (scan/store time, not WRR-creation time) and the receipt-commit transaction boundary (per line, not per WRR) — both are application-logic/workflow changes, not schema changes.

**This pass is spec-documents only.** `specs/07-incoming-receiving/requirements.md`, `design.md`, and `tasks.md`, and `specs/18-barcode-integration/requirements.md` and `design.md` were amended (see below). **No application code, migration, or Drizzle schema file was touched.** The following already-shipped files implement the now-superseded 2026-08-09 model and require follow-up rework as a separate implementation task, not done here:

- `lib/receiving/wrr-schema.ts` — currently requires `putawayLocationId` at line-creation time for `store` disposition; must instead accept it as optional/absent at staging and require it only at the per-line "Store" commit.
- `lib/receiving/scan-matcher.ts` — needs the new flow-type cross-check rejection reason (item `flow_type` vs. WRR `flow_type`) added to its rejection set.
- `lib/receiving/commit-validation.ts` — `validateCommit` currently requires ALL lines fully scanned/resolved before ANY line can commit; must be reworked to validate and commit one line at a time.
- `lib/actions/receiving.ts` — the confirm-receipt server action currently commits the whole WRR atomically; needs a per-line "Store"/"Hold" command replacing (or sitting alongside, during migration) the current end-of-WRR confirm command.
- `app/(authenticated)/receiving/new/_components/wrr-line-items.tsx` — currently collects `putawayLocationId` per `store` line at WRR-creation time in the office form; that field's collection point moves to the floor scan screen (not yet built for this purpose) and should be removed from this office-side form.

**What actually changed, in one place:**

1. **No manual flow-type selection at scan time.** `flow_type` is already required on `wrr_documents` at WRR creation and on each `items` row at enrollment (`01-core-data-model` design.md §5.7). The floor scan screen does not ask staff to pick a flow. A scanned item's own `flow_type` is now cross-checked against the WRR's `flow_type`; a mismatch rejects through the same exception path `07` design.md §6 already defines for wrong-WRR/wrong-item scans — an additive rejection reason, not a new UI step.
2. **Scan-first, then a system-suggested location — not pre-selected before scanning.** For a `store`-disposition line: scan → matches expected WRR line (including the new flow-type check) → system computes and displays a suggested location using the already-approved "location/capacity suggestion interface" (`07` design.md §10, unchanged in its own data contract — remaining CBM vs. candidate `locations`) → staff accepts or overrides → taps "Store". `wrr_items.putaway_location_id` is now populated at this step, not at WRR staging.
3. **Per-line immediate commit, not one atomic end-of-WRR commit.** Each line commits (creates its `lots` row, `lot_location_balances` row, and immutable `inventory_transactions` row with `movement_type = 'receiving'`) as its own atomic step, immediately on "Store" or "Hold." This generalizes a pattern already accepted in the 2026-08-09 "Eight cross-spec PO decisions" WRR-cancellation resolution (Option C), which already established that a WRR can reach a state where some lines are posted and others are not — there, only as a cancellation edge case; here, as the normal path for every WRR. **Left as an explicit open item, not resolved unilaterally**: `01-core-data-model`'s actual `wrr_status` enum (`staged_pending_arrival`, `receiving_in_progress`, `confirmed`, `cancelled`) has no `partial` value, despite the 2026-08-09 cancellation entry's own prose referencing a WRR "closing with `partial` status" — that value was never actually added to the schema. Whether `receiving_in_progress` is reused as-is for "some lines posted, not all," or `01` needs a new enum value, is `01`'s own amendment/approval process to resolve, not decided here.
4. **On-hold (`inspect`-disposition) lines: location-first, not scan-first.** Staff selects/confirms the (typically fixed, small) active `inspection` location first, then scans, then taps "Hold" for the same immediate per-line commit as `store`. Not inconsistent with point 2: the putaway suggestion needs the item's CBM/quantity to check storage capacity; a hold/quarantine location is a real but small fixed set of enrolled `locations` rows (`location_type = 'inspection'`) with no capacity-suggestion math involved, so there's no reason to gate confirming it behind a scan.
5. **`18-barcode-integration` gains WRR-time per-unit printed labels.** For a WRR line whose vendor supplies cartons without usable/trusted barcodes, the system can generate and print `N` (= `expected_qty`) labels at WRR creation/staging time, all sharing the line's item identity but each with its own unique per-unit identifier (`{"type": "wrr_item_unit", "wrr_item_id": "uuid", "unit_id": "uuid"}`) — not `N` identical bare-item-code labels. Grounded in `07` design.md §6's existing duplicate/over-quantity scan rejection: identical labels would make an exact-duplicate rescan indistinguishable from a legitimate second unit, degrading duplicate detection from exact to a fuzzy running count. Follows this project's existing unique-per-artifact pattern (`WAN:<uuid>` advance-notice labels already in `18`/`22`, the `(event_id, recipient_id, channel, template_version)` key in `14`, `generated_documents.snapshot_hash` in `10`). Distinct from `18` design.md §2.1's existing "Print Label" flow, which generates one label per already-enrolled lot/item at enrollment time — this is `N` per-unit labels per WRR line, before any lot exists, keyed to the WRR line/item rather than `dsw_id`/lot.

## Retention policy revised: permanent retention via hot/cold tiering, not indefinite Supabase storage (2026-08-09)

The Product Owner overturned the three previously recorded 3-year *deletion* decisions: `audit_log`, `lot_history_export`, and generated PDF/document artifacts (`generated_documents` and the underlying Storage objects: WRR, pick-list, acknowledgement-receipt, statement, and report PDFs) are **never deleted, ever**. However, "never deleted" is implemented as **tiered storage, not indefinite growth inside Supabase**:

- **Hot tier (0–3 years): stays in Supabase**, exactly as originally designed — `audit_log` rows live in Postgres, `generated_documents`-linked Storage objects live in the `generated-documents` bucket. Query/report/preview paths are unchanged.
- **Cold tier (3 years+): moved to a secondary off-platform archive** (e.g. S3/Backblaze — exact provider is an open infra choice, not decided here), via a scheduled job. The Supabase copy (DB row or Storage object) is only removed from the hot tier **after the archive write is verified** (hash match, per the existing `04` §10.4 pattern already used for the daily/PITR + yearly-archival backup flow). The data itself is never lost — it moves, it does not disappear. This keeps Supabase Postgres table size and Storage bucket cost bounded to a 3-year rolling window instead of growing without bound for the life of the product, while still satisfying "nothing is ever actually deleted."
- Anything already reachable via the archive-and-verify move is retrievable on request (restore-on-demand), same as any other archival backup — it is just not part of the live/hot query path.

This supersedes:

- The 2026-08-06 PO decision recorded in the "Eight cross-spec PO decisions resolved" entry above (`audit_log` 3-year retention **and deletion**) — the 3-year figure survives as the *hot-tier* window, only the "delete after 3 years" part is replaced with "archive after 3 years."
- Decision 2 in "Spec 10 PDF pipeline decisions" below (3-year retention + scheduled deletion for `generated_documents` rows and their Storage objects). **The nightly pg_cron reconciliation sweep is unaffected and still runs** — it still flips stuck `pending`/`generating` rows to `failed` after 4 hours and still deletes true orphans (Storage objects with no matching DB row, e.g. from a crashed retry — those were never valid artifacts to begin with, so "never delete valid data" doesn't apply to them). What changes is that the 3-year mark now triggers an **archive-and-verify move**, not a delete. The open sub-question this entry previously left open ("second cron step or Storage lifecycle rule — PO to confirm") is answered: neither — it's a job that copies to the secondary archive destination and verifies before removing the Supabase copy, using the same mechanism as `04`'s existing yearly archival backup job, not a Supabase Storage lifecycle rule (which only deletes, it cannot move-with-verification to an external destination).

Stage 2 dispatch rejects and marks `expired` in real time when `expires_at` is in the past. ## Spec 10 PDF pipeline decisions: inline generation + nightly cron cleanup (2026-08-09)

Two named PO decisions that were blocking Tasks 3–6 of `specs/10-pick-list-and-acknowledgement-receipt/tasks.md` are now resolved.

**Decision 1 — Inline vs. Edge/job PDF generation: inline (Option A).**
The PDF renderer runs synchronously inside the Server Action that fires the trigger (`commitWithdrawal` for pick lists, `dispatchPickList` for acknowledgement receipts). The `generated_documents` row flips from `pending` → `generated` in the same round-trip. No separate Edge Function, pg_cron job, or background queue is introduced for generation. Rationale: document volume in a single-warehouse VMI/Trading/Supplies operation is low; the simpler path is correct at this scale. The existing non-fatal try/catch already ensures a render failure cannot roll back inventory. If render latency becomes a problem at higher volumes, migration to Option B (async job) is additive — the `pending` row and `correlation_id` column already support it.

**Decision 2 — Orphan-artifact reconciliation: scheduled nightly cleanup (Option A).**
A nightly pg_cron job scans `generated_documents` rows stuck in `pending` or `generating` for more than 4 hours and marks them `failed`. Supabase Storage objects with no matching `generated_documents` row (i.e., DB write succeeded but artifact was written by a crashed retry) are identified by listing the `documents` bucket and cross-referencing against the DB; orphaned Storage objects are deleted. This runs as a SECURITY DEFINER SQL function callable by pg_cron. Retention: three years for DB rows (matching `audit_log` and `lot_history_export` decisions); Storage artifacts deleted at the same time as their DB row's three-year mark via a second cron step or Supabase Storage lifecycle rule — PO to confirm which mechanism. The 4-hour window (not 24-hour) keeps the attention state in `/documents` from lagging too long; supervisors see `failed` rows as attention items rather than permanent orphans.

These decisions unlock Tasks 3–6 in `10/tasks.md`. `tasks.md` updated to reflect resolved items.

## `lib/db/client.ts` broke every real Vercel build; fixed with lazy connection init (2026-08-08)

The actual Vercel deploy (commit `4a0c9ca`) failed during "Collecting page data" — `Failed to collect page data for /approvals/[approvalId]`, `TypeError: Invalid URL`. Traced to `lib/db/client.ts`: `postgres(connectionString, { prepare: false })` ran at module scope, and `postgres-js` parses its connection-string argument synchronously, throwing on an empty string rather than deferring to first query. The file's own comment claimed the opposite ("not validated at import time so `next build` can succeed without a live DB") — that claim was aspirational, not actually true of the code beneath it. **Fixed** by wrapping `db` in a `Proxy` that defers the real `postgres()`/`drizzle()` construction until the first property access, rather than at import time — every existing `import { db } from "@/lib/db/client"` call site keeps working completely unchanged (same named export, same shape); only the timing of the real connection attempt moves from "module import" to "first query," which happens only inside actual request handling (Server Component render, Server Action, route handler), never during static page-data collection.

## `0008_rls_policies.sql` still missing `GRANT CREATE ON SCHEMA rbac_internal TO rbac_definer` after an earlier attempted fix (2026-08-08)

Running `db:migrate` against the real, linked Supabase project failed at `ALTER FUNCTION rbac_internal.current_user_is_active() OWNER TO rbac_definer` with `permission denied for schema rbac_internal (SQLSTATE 42501)`. This is the second time this exact error surfaced: an earlier commit (`9f05b55`, "fix(track-a): rbac_definer grant, vitest timeout, and db client mock") already attempted a fix by moving `GRANT rbac_definer TO postgres` inside the migration's idempotent `DO` block — but that addressed role *membership* idempotency, not the actual missing privilege, so the same error reappeared on the next real run.

Root cause, confirmed by Postgres's actual `ALTER ... OWNER TO` privilege rule: the new owner of an object must hold `CREATE` on that object's containing schema — a separate check from "session role is a member of the new owner role." `0008` granted `rbac_definer` `USAGE` on `rbac_internal` but never `CREATE`. Against a real Postgres superuser (the local Docker container this migration was verified against during Phase 2), the check never fires — superuser bypasses it entirely — which is why two rounds of `db-migration-verifier`/`rbac-rls-reviewer` real-Postgres passes never caught this. Supabase's hosted `postgres` role isn't a true superuser (a fact the migration's own comment on `GRANT rbac_definer TO postgres` already anticipated, just not completely), so the check fires there and only there.

**Fixed** by adding `GRANT CREATE ON SCHEMA rbac_internal TO rbac_definer;` immediately after the existing `USAGE` grant, with an inline comment explaining both the mechanism and why local verification missed it. Confirmed safe to simply re-run: `drizzle-orm`'s postgres-js migrator wraps an entire migration file in one `session.transaction(...)` (`node_modules/drizzle-orm/pg-core/dialect.js`), and `CREATE ROLE`/`CREATE SCHEMA`/`CREATE FUNCTION` are all transactional DDL in Postgres, so the failed attempt rolled back completely — no partial state was left on the real project.

Fixed directly in `0008_rls_policies.sql` rather than as a new fix-forward migration (the way `0009_lot_inventory_totals_grant.sql` fixed `0002`'s view): `0009`'s target had already successfully applied to a real, persistent database elsewhere; `0008` has never successfully completed against any real, persistent database — only ephemeral test containers — so there is no shipped state anywhere for a fix-forward migration to reconcile against. This is a correction to unreleased content, not a patch to something already live.

**Same bug, second occurrence, same day**: the fix above was incomplete. Re-running the migration got one statement further and failed again — `ALTER VIEW public.party_visible_items OWNER TO rbac_definer` (statement 127) with `permission denied for schema public`, the identical mechanism, just a different target schema. `0008` grants `rbac_definer` `USAGE` on `public` but never `CREATE`, and `party_visible_items` (a view created later in the same migration) needs the same ownership reassignment as the five `rbac_internal` functions. ## Track 2 Phase A (`06`) — two open RBAC/RLS architectural gaps recorded; not Track 2's to fix unilaterally (2026-08-08)

During the `rbac-rls-reviewer` pass over the Track 2 Phase A (`06-party-and-item-enrollment`) implementation, two structural gaps were found and documented here rather than silently fixed, because both require architectural decisions outside Track 2's authority.

**Gap A — Drizzle client bypasses RLS entirely (`lib/db/client.ts`).** The project's Drizzle client is a plain `postgres-js` connection using `DATABASE_URL`, which in a Supabase setup is typically the service role or Postgres superuser credential — both carry `BYPASSRLS`. RLS provides no independent backstop for any missed server-side path. **Resolution deferred to `04-services-and-infrastructure`** as the spec that finalizes the database connection mode (direct Drizzle vs. Supabase session client vs. the §6.3 transaction wrapper). Track 1 is the sole writer of shared infrastructure files; Track 2 opened a named cross-track request rather than editing `lib/db/client.ts` directly. The application-layer `requirePermission()` gates are present and correct on all mutation and read paths in Phase A — this is a defense-in-depth gap, not an unguarded path.

**Gap B — `addPartyRole` / `removePartyRole` and deactivate mutations use direct Drizzle, not controlled `SECURITY DEFINER` service operations.** `02-rbac-roles/design.md` §7.4 states that `party_roles` mutations should go through controlled service operations that independently re-verify capability at the database layer. Since Gap A shows RLS is bypassed anyway, the default-deny-for-mutations has no effect on these calls. **Resolution also deferred to `04`** for the same connection-mode reason — implementing a `SECURITY DEFINER` wrapper for `party_roles` mutations would require a new migration in `supabase/migrations/` (a Track 1 locked file). The application-layer check is correct and present; the database has no backstop if that check is ever missed. This matches the same class of gap documented in `02-rbac-roles`'s own `§8` defense-in-depth model.

**What Track 2 did fix in this pass**: corrected `lib/auth/page-resolver.ts`'s `user_party_scopes` and `user_roles` queries to add the `valid_from ≤ now()` / `(valid_until IS NULL OR valid_until > now())` interval conditions, which were present in `0008`'s `rbac_internal.has_party_scope` RLS helper but absent from the application-layer resolver — a real divergence that would have granted party scope to not-yet-active or already-expired assignments. ## Clarification: `12`/`13`'s calculation logic is downstream of manually-encoded item-enrollment fields, not an independent pricing/order system (2026-08-08)

The Product Owner flagged the relationship between item enrollment (`06`) and the two billing/pricing specs for explicit confirmation: `12-vmi-billing` and `13-trading-orders-and-pricing` are **calculation engines that consume fields captured at item enrollment**, not systems that independently define price or CBM. Checked both designs against this framing — no contradiction found, both already implement it correctly, so this entry records the confirmed reading rather than a fix.

- **VMI side**: `items.volume_cbm` (`01-core-data-model/design.md` line 263, `decimal("volume_cbm")`, manually encoded per item at `06` enrollment) is the sole per-item input `12`'s nightly ledger job multiplies against `lot_inventory_totals.qty_remaining` to get `ending_cbm` (`12-vmi-billing/design.md` §2.1, step 2). `12` never stores or re-derives CBM itself — it is purely `SUM(qty_remaining × items.volume_cbm)`, then `period_average_cbm × contract.cbm_rate_usd × storage_period_days` at statement time (§2.2 step 4). The per-document (pick-list/receipt) price is explicitly never the VMI bill, per `CLAUDE.md`'s non-negotiable-decisions list — `12` only ever reads the CBM ledger.
- **Trading side**: `items.buying_price` and `items.selling_price` (`01-core-data-model/design.md` lines 255-256, both manually encoded at `06` enrollment) are reference-only inputs. `13-trading-orders-and-pricing/design.md` §3 states this explicitly: `items.selling_price` is "reference data only," never the final transaction price. The authoritative computation happens per-order in `trading_price_snapshots.effective_price = unit_price × (1 + tax_rate/100) × (1 − discount_rate/100)`, frozen and hashed at `price_set_ready` (§3-4). `unit_price` is set by an authorized user at order time (`trading.price_set` capability) using the item's enrolled `selling_price` as a starting reference, not as the computed output itself.

Net effect for both: the enrollment-time fields are hand-entered inputs; `12`/`13`'s entire job is to compute derived outputs (period-average storage charge; tax/discount-adjusted effective price) from those inputs plus operational data (inventory totals, contract rates, order lines) — never to redefine or bypass what was manually encoded at enrollment. No schema or design change was made; both `12` and `13` remain `Status: Approved`. ## `02` RBAC/RLS design review — one blocking security gap, several underspecified mechanisms (2026-08-05)

Ran `rbac-rls-reviewer` against `02`'s design.md (no code exists yet; this is a pre-implementation document review). The three-layer UI/server/RLS enforcement posture and most §7.4 patterns (`wrr_documents`, `pick_lists`, parent-inheriting `wrr_items`/`pick_list_items`) were assessed as sound and genuinely enforced-at-data-layer in intent, not just assumed. Six findings needed fixing, all now resolved in design.md:

1. **Blocking security gap**: §3.2 stated null-`flow_type` party scope means "all externally applicable flows," excluding Supplies — but §7.2's `has_party_scope` helper was described as "honoring null as all allowed flows" with no mechanism actually excluding `'supplies'`, and no capability-model rule stopped Supplies resources from being modeled as `assigned_party`-scoped in the first place. A party user with a null-`flow_type` scope on a party that is also a Supplies vendor could pass `has_party_scope(vendor_party_id, 'supplies')` and read internal Supplies data — directly violating requirements.md FR-4.2 and acceptance criterion #7. This was intent-only prose with no enforced mechanism, the exact failure class this review exists to catch. **Fixed** with two independent, redundant gates: `has_party_scope`'s own match logic now explicitly excludes `'supplies'` from null-match; `can_access_party_resource` independently hard-denies any `assigned_party`-scope check where the target row's `flow_type = 'supplies'`, regardless of what `has_party_scope` alone returns. The capability catalog rule is now explicit: Supplies-flow capabilities MUST be `scope_kind = 'global'`, never `assigned_party`.
2. **`lots`/`inventory_transactions` had one uniform `owner_party_id`-based RLS pattern**, but `owner_party_id` is required-and-authoritative for VMI while optional-and-not-the-customer for Trading (the warehouse owns Trading stock pre-sale; the same lot can split across multiple customer pick lists). A uniform policy would either wrongly deny Trading customers their own picked lot or leak an unrelated party's lot. **Fixed**: explicit two-branch policy — VMI uses `owner_party_id`; Trading joins through `pick_list_items.lot_id` → `pick_lists.customer_party_id`; Supplies is never party-scoped (per finding 1's rule). `inventory_transactions` inherits the same branch since it has no direct party column.
3. **`items` party-visibility had no join path specified at all** (unlike every other §7.4 row, which names exact columns). **Fixed**: party-user item visibility is now defined as derived only through `item_id` values reachable via the caller's own scoped `wrr_items`/`pick_list_items` records, excluding `default_supplier_party_id`/pricing fields from that projection — never a direct catalog read, and `default_supplier_party_id` is explicitly barred from ever being used as an RLS predicate (it identifies the warehouse's supplier, not the requesting party).
4. **`SECURITY DEFINER` helper owner/grant strategy was underspecified** — unclear how the helpers read RLS-protected `role_permissions`/`user_party_scopes` without either breaking (too-narrow grants) or over-granting (bypassing everything). **Fixed**: named a dedicated `rbac_definer` owner role with `BYPASSRLS` and table-scoped `SELECT` only on the specific tables these four functions read; ordinary roles get `EXECUTE` on the functions, never direct table access. Also fixed: these helpers must never be exposed as client-callable Supabase RPC endpoints (boolean-oracle enumeration risk) — added an explicit no-RPC-exposure requirement and a real-Postgres test for it.
5. **Last-administrator invariant lock (§8.5) was imprecise enough to permit a still-racy "spec-compliant" implementation**: a per-row lock on only the target administrator being revoked does not serialize against the aggregate count — two concurrent revocations of two *different* administrators can each independently see "1 will remain" and both commit, leaving zero. **Fixed**: specified a single fixed-key `pg_advisory_xact_lock` on a constant identifying the invariant itself, serializing all such operations globally. Also fixed: §8.3 (role revocation) didn't reference the invariant at all, even though FR-7.6 requires it for revocation as well as deactivation, and revoking the `administrator` role is a distinct code path from deactivating the profile — added the missing cross-reference.
6. Minor: §8's admin operations now state explicitly that privileged server functions must independently re-verify the caller's capability rather than trusting an upstream `requirePermission()` call; §6.3's transaction wrapper now states rollback must be guaranteed via try/finally on callback exception.

`02`'s Sign-off section remains fully unchecked pending real-Postgres tests of these fixes (now added to §13's testing design) and both approver sign-offs.

## `02` real-Postgres verification — 4 implementation-detail bugs found and fixed (2026-08-05)

Ran `db-migration-verifier` against `02`'s design with real disposable Postgres 16, hand-building stand-in `01` tables since no actual migrations exist yet. Six items requested, all PASS — but 4 real bugs surfaced only by actually running the DDL and invoking the functions, not by reading them, matching the class of bug this project has already been burned by (CBM/unit mismatches, library-version pins):

1. §4.6's `COALESCE(flow_type::text, '__sentinel__')` partial-unique-index approach fails outright — the implicit enum→text cast is `STABLE`, not `IMMUTABLE`, and Postgres rejects non-immutable expressions in an index. **Fixed**: switched to Postgres 15+'s native `NULLS NOT DISTINCT` index modifier, which is both correct and simpler than the original approach.
2. The four `SECURITY DEFINER` helpers all call `auth.uid()`, but `rbac_definer` had no grant to execute it — every call would fail with `permission denied for schema auth`.
3. `can_access_party_resource` calls other `rbac_internal`-qualified functions internally, but `rbac_definer` had no `USAGE` on that schema — `permission denied for schema rbac_internal`.
4. The admin mutation functions (owned by `rbac_admin_ops`, per §8's defense-in-depth re-verification requirement) call `rbac_internal.has_permission` internally, but had no `EXECUTE` grant on it — this broke the **entire admin mutation path**, caught on the very first concurrency test run, not an edge case.

All three grant gaps (2-4) are now folded into §7.2's owner/privilege strategy as explicit required grants, not left as implementation-time discoveries. The concurrency and Supplies-leak scenarios themselves — the actual security properties, as opposed to these DDL/grant mechanics — verified PASS with real concurrent Postgres backends and real row-level SELECT evidence, including a control experiment that reproduced the exact last-administrator race §8.5 exists to prevent when using only the row-level lock the design calls insufficient.

**Separately, real-Postgres review surfaced a genuine design contradiction** in the `items`/`party_visible_items` masking view (§7.4, from an earlier fix round): specifying it as a `security_invoker` view was backwards. A `security_invoker` view is subject to the base table's own RLS on top of its own filter — since party users correctly have zero direct grant on `items`, a `security_invoker` view would always return zero rows, defeating its purpose; loosening `items`' RLS to fix that would let a direct query on a now-visible row leak the very columns the view exists to hide. **Fixed**: the view must be a default (non-`security_invoker`) view owned by the same privileged role as the RBAC helpers, with the party-scope filter embedded in the view's own `WHERE` clause — Postgres evaluates a plain view's table access under the *owner's* privileges, not the querying session's, which is exactly the property needed here.

`02`'s Sign-off now has requirements/design review, `rbac-rls-reviewer`, and `db-migration-verifier` all checked; only remaining items are a follow-up real-Postgres pass on the explicitly-listed untested pieces (PostgREST introspection, the `party_visible_items` view itself, the full three-way `lots` matrix, pooler JWT propagation, revoke-then-access flow) and both approver signatures.

The nullable staging column is added by `0020_wrr_item_putaway_location.sql`; the confirmation invariant, rather than a cross-row database CHECK, makes it required specifically for `store` lines. `01` and `07` documentation have been amended to reflect the decision. No `warehouse_id`, new capability string, or duplicate stock ledger is introduced.

## Batch receiving / split putaway amendment (2026-08-20) — pending reapproval

The Product Owner requested a lower-friction receiving workflow for cartons or pallets that may be placed in different locations: one accepted QR can open a batch placement surface, then staff can assign the declared physical quantity across locations instead of scanning every carton merely to enter a location.

The amendment is intentionally bounded:

- Printed QR labels remain unique and may be applied in any physical order. Batch allocation does not alter their payloads and does not claim individual-label reconciliation from one scan.
- Batch allocation presents unsequenced placement slots (or grouped quantities), with each storage location's used/maximum CBM, remaining CBM, projected remaining CBM, and contents preview.

- `store` creates one business lot but one `lot_location_balances` row and one immutable receiving transaction per selected location. `inspect` uses an active inspection location and **Hold All**, without storage allocation.
